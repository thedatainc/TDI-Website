The challenge

A service problem can appear in several systems: a performance counter in one, an incident ticket in another, and a separate customer-impact report. Different identifiers and reporting intervals make comparisons difficult. Analysts spend time reconciling extracts before they can investigate the underlying issue.

How OMNInet works

A common data layer aligns site identifiers, timestamps and service definitions. Quality checks flag missing or delayed feeds. Dashboards connect performance changes to incident history, with drill-down by region, site and service. Automated summaries direct attention to exceptions rather than replace engineering judgement.

In practice

A regional service metric deteriorates repeatedly at similar times. An analyst opens the affected sites, compares their incident history and identifies which cases need engineering investigation, without rebuilding the dataset in a spreadsheet.

Capabilities
  • Source mapping, ingestion and a consistent operational data model
  • Checks for missing feeds, duplicates and inconsistent identifiers
  • Performance dashboards with incident context and exception queues
  • Report definitions and access controls shared across operational teams
How a pilot is measured

A pilot would test data completeness, reporting freshness, reconciliation effort and time to assemble an investigation. These are proposed evaluation measures; no customer deployment, outage reduction or business result is asserted.
